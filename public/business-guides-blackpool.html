Why we wrote these guides

Most people who message us about a unit are not property experts. They are makers, repairers, online sellers and tradespeople who have outgrown a spare room or a garage. The questions they ask are rarely about square feet. They want to know whether they will pay business rates, whether they need insurance, how to get on Google Maps, or whether to set up as a sole trader first.

These guides answer those questions in one place. They are written for small, local businesses, not corporations, and they try to be honest about what is simple and what needs proper advice. Where a rule applies, we link to GOV.UK, HSE, HMRC, Companies House or Blackpool Council so you can check the current position yourself.

If you are at the very beginning, start here. These guides walk through the first decisions and the paperwork that follows.

Choosing and using premises

Where you work shapes your costs, your hours and how customers see you. These guides help you weigh up the options and make the most of the space you pick.

  • Moving your business out of home: the signs it is time, the costs to compare and a calm plan for the move itself.
  • Business unit vs shop vs coworking: an honest comparison of the three most common next steps for a small business.
  • How to set up a small workshop: layout, power, storage walls and dust, for anyone making or fixing things.
  • Working in a windowless unit: lighting, air, breaks and the small habits that make an enclosed space comfortable.

Money, rules and protection

These are the topics people worry about most, and the ones where a little knowledge goes a long way. Each guide explains the basics and points you to the official source.

  • Business rates for small units: what rateable value means, how Small Business Rate Relief works and who to ask.
  • Small business insurance guide: the covers small firms commonly consider, and the one that is compulsory once you employ people.
  • Health and safety in a small unit: risk assessments, fire safety, electrics and working alone, scaled to a one or two person business.

Finding customers

A unit is only useful if people can find you. These guides cover being visible online and selling face to face around town.

  • Google Business Profile address guide: how to list a unit address properly so you show up in local searches without breaking Google's rules.
  • Selling at Blackpool markets: how market trading works, what the council licenses and how to store your stall kit between events.
  • Running a seasonal business in Blackpool: planning around the visitor season and the Illuminations, and getting through the quiet months.

How to use these guides

Read them in any order. Each one stands on its own, and they link to each other where a topic overlaps. If you only have ten minutes, these three cover the most ground:

  1. Get the structure rightDecide how you will trade and register with HMRC in good time. The sole trader guide explains the choice.
  2. Know your running costsAdd rent, electricity, insurance and any business rates together before you commit to premises. The rates guide explains what to ask.
  3. Make yourself findableOnce you have an address, set up your Google Business Profile properly so local customers can find you.

The guides are general. They will not replace an accountant, an insurance broker or a solicitor when your situation is unusual, but they should help you ask those people better questions.

Before you act on this

Everything in these guides is general information, not legal, tax or insurance advice. Rules, thresholds and fees change, often at the start of a new tax year in April, so always check the official guidance linked from each page, or speak to a qualified adviser, before you make a decision that depends on it.

If something on these pages looks out of date, please tell us and we will put it right.

Questions

Are these guides only for Rock Factory tenants?

No. They are free for anyone running or starting a small business in Blackpool and the Fylde coast. Where a guide mentions our units, it is clearly marked.

Do the guides replace an accountant or solicitor?

No. They explain the basics and link to official sources such as GOV.UK and HSE. For decisions that depend on your own figures or circumstances, speak to a qualified adviser.

Which guide should I read first?

If you have not registered your business yet, start with how to start a business in Blackpool. If you are already trading from home, read moving your business out of home.

How often are the guides updated?

We review them when official guidance changes, especially around the start of each tax year. Always check the linked official page for the current position.

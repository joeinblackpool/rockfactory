Storage guide

How to get cheap storage
in Blackpool

The cheapest storage is rarely the one with the lowest headline price. It is the one that is the right size, on the right terms, holding only things worth keeping. Here is how to get there.

1. Stop paying to store air

The most common way to overpay for storage is not a bad price. It is a unit that is too big. Empty floor space costs exactly the same as full floor space.

Most people size up out of caution, then pay for that caution every month. Make a list of the large items, estimate the boxes, and pack upwards: stacked boxes use height you are already paying for.

Our unit size guide turns square feet into rooms and boxes, and how to pack a storage unit shows how to fit more into less.

2. Compare price per square foot, not per unit

A small unit nearly always looks cheaper and is nearly always more expensive per square foot. To compare fairly, work out the yearly cost per square foot: weekly price × 52 ÷ size.

3. Convert everything to the same period

Some prices are quoted weekly, some monthly, some per four weeks. Four weeks is not a month: a year has twelve months but thirteen four-week periods. Convert every quote to a monthly figure (weekly × 52 ÷ 12) before you compare.

We quote weekly prices and show the monthly figure next to them, so you never have to do this sum in your head at the counter.

4. Ask what is not included

The headline price is only part of the bill. Before you sign anywhere, ask about:

  • Deposits, and when you get them back.
  • Insurance, and whether it is compulsory.
  • Padlocks, admin fees and set-up charges.
  • Price rises after an introductory offer ends.
  • The notice period, and what happens if you leave early.

Here, the deposit is one month's rent, the minimum term is one month and notice is one month. Electricity in ground-floor units is charged at the supplier rate with no markup.

5. Do not pay to store a decision

Storage is brilliant for things you will use again. It is an expensive way to postpone deciding about things you will not. A sideboard nobody wants costs nothing in the loft and something every month in a unit.

Spend one afternoon sorting before you move anything. Sell, donate or recycle what you would not pay to keep. Our decluttering guide has a simple method.

6. Match the term to the job

Long contracts can look cheaper per month, but only if you really need the full term. If you need space for a house move or a renovation, a rolling monthly agreement means you stop paying the month the job is done.

See short-term storage in Blackpool for how to plan it.

7. Share, or swap as you go

Two households or two small businesses can split one unit, as long as you both trust each other and one person is the named tenant. And if your needs shrink, ask to move to a smaller space rather than carrying on paying for the bigger one. At The Rock Factory you can swap units any time, subject to availability.

Questions

What is the cheapest storage you offer?

From spring 2027, first-floor storage pods at guide prices from £10 a week for 15 sq ft. Our ground-floor units start at £65 a week for 150 sq ft.

Is a bigger unit better value?

Per square foot, usually yes. A 100 sq ft room at the guide price works out at about £17.68 per sq ft per year, against about £34.67 for a 15 sq ft pod. It is only better value if you would fill it.

How do I compare weekly and monthly prices?

Multiply the weekly price by 52 and divide by 12. For example, £65 a week is about £282 a month.

Are there hidden fees?

The deposit is one month's rent, refundable under our rental terms. Electricity in ground-floor units is charged at the supplier rate with no markup.

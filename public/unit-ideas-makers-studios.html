Picture framing

A framing workshop fits in a 150 or 180 sq ft unit with upright racks for mouldings, board and glass along one wall.

  • Power needLow
  • LayoutModerate
  • Set-up costMedium
  • Best unit150 sq ft or 180 sq ft

A mitre saw or guillotine, an underpinner and mount cutter, with vertical racks for moulding lengths, mount board and glass.

Technical profile

Equipment that fits
  • Mitre guillotine or mitre saw
  • Underpinner
  • Mount cutter
  • Glass cutting table
  • Vertical racks for moulding lengths, board and glass
Power and utilities

Most framing tools are manual or pneumatic. A mitre saw and a small compressor run on standard sockets.

Safety and site rules

Wood, board and glass are low-risk materials. Store glass upright in a rack, never flat on the floor.

Floor plan

Vertical racking
Moulding lengths stand upright in a wall rack, board and glass in slotted racks, and finished frames are wrapped and stacked by the door.
Mobile benches
A wheeled assembly bench can be pulled out for large frames and pushed back against the wall for everyday work.
Flow of goods
Mouldings and board come in through the shutter to their racks. Each job is cut, mounted, glazed and assembled, then wrapped and placed by the door for collection.

  • Wrapping materials

Small-scale woodworking

Hand tools and benchtop machines with dust extraction fit in a 180 or 300 sq ft roller-shutter unit, and wood is a low-risk material.

  • Power needHigher
  • LayoutComplex
  • Set-up costMedium
  • Best unit180 sq ft or 300 sq ft

Hand tools and benchtop machines for chopping boards, boxes, frames, signs and turned items. Wood is a low-risk material and fine to store and work.

Technical profile

Equipment that fits
  • Heavy workbench with vice
  • Benchtop bandsaw or scroll saw
  • Benchtop pillar drill
  • Mitre saw on a mobile stand
  • Dust extractor or workshop vacuum
  • Timber wall rack
Power and utilities

Benchtop machines run on single-phase power, which is what the units have. Choose single-phase machines and avoid running two motors at once on the same socket. There is no three-phase power.

Safety and site rules

Wood is low-risk to store and work. Fine dust is the main issue, so use extraction and clear offcuts daily. HSE has guidance on wood dust. Ask us before using solvent-based finishes in any quantity.

Floor plan

Vertical racking
Timber on a wall rack with long lengths low and offcuts in a bin, clamps on a wall rail, and hand tools on a pegboard above the bench.
Mobile benches
Put benchtop machines on wheeled stands with locking castors so they can be rolled out to use and pushed back to keep the floor clear.
Flow of goods
Timber comes in through the shutter to the rack, is cut at the machines, assembled at the bench, sanded with extraction running, then finished and packed near the door.

Launch checklist

Start-up supplies
  • Workbench
  • Two or three benchtop machines
  • Dust extraction
  • Clamps and hand tools
  • Timber rack
  • Dust masks and ear defenders
Insurance to discuss

Ask a broker about contents cover for tools and machines, products liability for items you sell, and public liability if customers visit.

Planning a small unit floor

Go up the walls, keep benches mobile and give goods one clear route from the door to the work area and back.

  • Measure before you buy racking. Ask us for the height of the unit you are viewing, then choose shelving that uses it safely, fixed to the wall where the manufacturer says so.
  • Heavy low, light high. Keep heavy stock and machines at or below waist height and light, bulky items on the top shelves.
  • Lockable castors. Benches and rails on lockable wheels let one room switch between making, photographing and packing.
  • One-way flow. Goods in at the door, work in the middle, finished orders back by the door. The 150, 180 and 300 sq ft units have roller shutters; the 160 sq ft unit has a security door.
  • Sockets first. Put machines near sockets and avoid chaining extension leads.

Why our units suit makers & creative studios

  • Wood, paper, card, canvas and metal hand-work are low-risk materials, so most dry making and creative work suits our units.
  • The ground floors are windowless, which helps for photography and video, where you want to control the light yourself.
  • You can leave your studio set up between sessions and lock up, instead of packing everything away at home.
  • Lighting, standard single-phase electricity and Wi-Fi are already connected in every unit (there is no three-phase power), with 24-hour access, free CCTV app access covering the entrance and the area outside the units, and shared facilities.

Not allowed in our units

Nothing that melts or heats combustible materials such as waxes, plastics, resins or oils, no flammable liquids or gases in bulk, and no car storage. Low-risk materials such as wood, paper, card, fabric and dry goods are fine. Every business use needs our approval first.

Start-up checklist

  1. Decide which unit suits your stock, machines and how you will load and unload.
  2. Message us with what your business does, so we can approve the use before you commit.
  3. Register your business with HMRC as a sole trader or limited company if you have not already.
  4. Speak to an insurance broker about public liability, contents and stock cover, and employers' liability if you have staff.
  5. Ask us whether business rates apply, and check Small Business Rate Relief on GOV.UK.
  6. Plan your layout: shelving up the walls, benches near sockets, and a clear route to the door.
  7. Arrange one month's rent, the deposit, photo ID and a Direct Debit mandate for move-in day.

Questions

Can I use power tools in the unit?

Hand tools and benchtop power tools are generally fine for dry materials such as wood, metal and card. Tell us what you plan to use when you enquire, because all business use needs our approval.

Is the unit suitable for photography?

The ground floors are windowless, which makes it easier to control the light for product photography and video. The 300 sq ft unit also has an upstairs room with a window.

Can I hold classes or workshops?

Tenants can receive clients at the unit. If you plan to run classes, tell us how many people you expect so we can check it suits the space.

What is included in the rent?

Lighting, electricity and Wi-Fi are already connected in every unit. Electricity usage is charged separately at the supplier rate, with no markup. Tenants get 24-hour access, free CCTV app access covering the entrance and the area outside the units, and shared facilities: a tea and coffee room, toilets and water.

Can I use the address on my Google Business Profile?

Yes. Tenants can use The Old Rock Factory, Keswick Road, Blackpool FY1 5PB as their business address on Google, so customers can find you without you putting your home address online.

Is there three-phase power?

No. Every unit has standard single-phase electricity, the same as a normal plug socket. Choose machines and tools that run on single-phase power.

What can't I do in a unit?

We do not allow anything that melts or heats combustible materials such as waxes, plastics, resins or oils, flammable liquids or gases in bulk, or car storage. Low-risk materials such as wood, paper, card, fabric and dry goods are fine. Every business use needs our approval.

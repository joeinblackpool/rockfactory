A seaside town's rhythm

Few places swing between seasons quite like a seaside resort. Through the warmer months, the town fills with visitors. Autumn brings the Illuminations, which extend the season for many local businesses. Then winter is quieter, and many people turn to repairs, refits and planning for next year.

That rhythm affects what people need to store. A guest house owner might need room for spare bedding and garden furniture in winter. A market trader might need more stock space ahead of the busy months. A family might want the paddling pool and barbecue out of the shed in October, and the Christmas tree out of the loft in November.

Rotating household kit

Most homes have two sets of things: what you use now, and what you will use in six months. Keeping both at home means cupboards that never close and a garage you cannot get into.

Seasonal stock for local businesses

Businesses that trade with visitors often need very different amounts of space through the year. Stock builds up before the busy months, then dwindles. Equipment used only in summer sits idle all winter.

  • Guest houses and holiday lets: spare linen, replacement furniture, garden and outdoor seating, decorating supplies for winter refits.
  • Market and event traders: gazebos, tables, display stands and stock between events.
  • Retailers: overflow stock ahead of the season, or end-of-season stock waiting for next year.
  • Event and entertainment businesses: props, sound gear and decorations.

Our guide to running a seasonal business in Blackpool, unit ideas for market traders and unit ideas for event hire go into more depth. All business use needs prior approval, so tell us what you plan to keep and do in the unit.

Flexing your space with the seasons

Rolling monthly terms make it easier to match space to the season. The minimum is one month, then you give one month's notice to leave. You can also swap to a bigger or smaller unit at any time, subject to availability.

That means a business could take a larger unit ahead of the season and drop down once the stock has sold. Swaps depend on what is free at the time, so plan ahead and let us know early. If you need more space than one unit, some have interconnecting doors, so you can rent two or three side by side.

Our unit comparison page sets out the options, from the 150 sq ft unit at £65 a week up to the 300 sq ft two-storey unit at £130 a week.

Winter refits for guest houses and holiday lets

The quieter months are when many accommodation owners redecorate, replace carpets or refit bathrooms. Clearing rooms of beds, wardrobes and soft furnishings while the work goes on keeps everything clean and lets trades work faster.

A roller-shutter unit suits this best because beds, mattresses and wardrobes load straight in. The 180 sq ft roller-shutter unit at £78 a week, about £338 a month, gives room for the contents of several guest rooms. Plan the order you will put rooms back together, and load the unit so the first rooms to reopen come out first.

Our renovation storage guide covers the planning in more detail, and the furniture storage guide explains how to wrap and stack beds and wardrobes.

Packing seasonal items so they last

  1. Clean and dry everythingSand, salt and damp are the enemies here. Rinse beach gear and let tents and cushions dry fully before packing.
  2. Remove fuel and gasEmpty lawnmowers and generators of fuel. Gas cylinders from barbecues and patio heaters must not be stored in bulk unless agreed in writing.
  3. Take batteries outBatteries left in decorations and toys can leak over months.
  4. Use labelled cratesLabel by season as well as contents, so swapping over is quick.
  5. Put next season at the frontWhatever you will need next goes nearest the door.

Christmas and one-off events

Christmas decorations are a classic seasonal store. Lights, wreaths and trees take up a surprising amount of loft or cupboard space for eleven months of the year. Keep fragile baubles in their original boxes or egg-box style trays, wrap lights around a piece of card to stop tangles, and store the tree in its bag or box.

The same applies to one-off events: a wedding, a big birthday or a community event. Decorations, signage and hired-in kit can be gathered in one place in the weeks before, so the day itself is easier to set up. Community groups and clubs often do the same with equipment between seasons, as our unit ideas for clubs and community groups page describes.

Access all year round

Seasons do not always arrive on schedule. A warm spell in April, a last-minute booking, an extra market date. With 24-hour access you can collect what you need when you need it, without waiting for opening times. Lighting and power are connected in every unit, so evening visits in the darker months are straightforward.

Questions

Can I store a barbecue?

Yes, once it is cleaned. Remove the gas cylinder first. Gas cylinders are not allowed in bulk unless agreed in writing.

Can I rent storage just for the winter?

Yes. The minimum term is one month and it then rolls monthly, so you can rent for as many months as you need and give one month's notice to leave.

Can I change unit size between seasons?

You can swap to a bigger or smaller unit at any time, subject to availability. Ask early if you know the busy season is coming.

Can I store stock for my seasonal business?

Yes, with our prior approval for the business use. Low-risk goods such as dry goods, textiles, wood and paper are fine. Cooking and hot food are not permitted.

Prices are subject to availability. Minimum rental term: one month, then one month’s notice to leave. Electricity usage is charged separately at the supplier rate, with no markup. Business use needs prior approval. Car storage is not permitted.

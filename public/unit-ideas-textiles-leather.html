Ideas and how to set them up

Tailoring and alterations

One or two sewing machines, an overlocker, a cutting table and rails for customers' garments fit in a 150 or 160 sq ft unit.

  • Power needMedium
  • LayoutModerate
  • Set-up costMedium
  • Best unit150 sq ft or 160 sq ft

Room for one or two sewing machines, an overlocker, a cutting table, a steam iron and rails for work in progress and finished garments.

Technical profile

Equipment that fits
  • Industrial lockstitch sewing machine with a servo motor
  • Overlocker
  • Cutting table at standing height
  • Steam iron or steam generator on a heat-proof stand
  • Rails for jobs in progress and finished work
  • Full-length mirror for fittings
Power and utilities

Industrial machines with servo motors run on standard single-phase sockets and draw little power. A steam generator iron draws the most, so give it its own socket. There is no three-phase power in the units.

Safety and site rules

Fabric and thread are low-risk materials. Use the iron on a heat-proof stand and switch it off at the end of the day. Every business use needs our approval first.

Floor plan

Vertical racking
Shelving above the machines for thread, zips and trims in labelled boxes. Hang customers' garments on rails tagged with name and collection date.
Mobile benches
Put the cutting table on lockable castors so it can be pushed against the wall to make room for a fitting, then rolled back out for cutting.
Flow of goods
Customers hand over garments at the door, which are tagged and hung on the in-progress rail. Work moves to the cutting table, then to the machines and the pressing station, and finished items go to a collection rail near the door.

Launch checklist

Start-up supplies
  • Sewing machine and overlocker
  • Cutting table
  • Iron and board
  • Two rails and hangers
  • Tags and job book
  • Mirror
  • Basic stock of thread and zips
Insurance to discuss

Customers' garments in your care are the key risk, so ask a broker about goods in trust cover, plus public liability for customers at fittings and contents cover for your machines.

Embroidery and personalisation

Single-head and small multi-head embroidery machines that run on a standard socket, plus blank stock on shelving, fit in a 150 or 180 sq ft unit.

  • Power needMedium
  • LayoutModerate
  • Set-up costHigh
  • Best unit150 sq ft or 180 sq ft

Commercial embroidery machines for logos on workwear, hats, team kit and gifts, plus stock of blank garments. Heat-press transfers can also work here; ask us about your machine.

Technical profile

Equipment that fits
  • Single-head or small multi-head commercial embroidery machine on its stand
  • Hooping station
  • Laptop with digitising software
  • Thread rack
  • Heat press for transfers, if we approve it
Power and utilities

Check that any machine you buy runs on single-phase power from a normal socket, because there is no three-phase supply. A heat press draws a lot of power, so give it its own socket and ask us about it first.

Safety and site rules

Stitching onto fabric is dry, mechanical work. A heat press heats transfers rather than melting combustible materials, but it is still a heat source, so tell us about it when you enquire.

Floor plan

Vertical racking
Blank garments on shelving by size and colour, thread on a wall rack above the machine, and finished orders bagged and labelled on a separate shelf.
Mobile benches
A wheeled hooping and folding table can sit beside the machine during a run and be moved to the door for bagging and dispatch.
Flow of goods
Blank stock comes in through the shutter to its shelf. Garments are hooped, embroidered, trimmed and folded, then bagged and labelled on the dispatch shelf by the door.

Launch checklist

Start-up supplies
  • Embroidery machine
  • Hoops and stabiliser
  • Thread in core colours
  • Digitising software
  • Shelving for blanks
  • Bags and labels
Insurance to discuss

Ask a broker about contents cover for the machine, which is usually the biggest single cost, goods in trust for customers' own garments, and products liability for items you sell.

Planning a small unit floor

Go up the walls, keep benches mobile and give goods one clear route from the door to the work area and back.

  • Measure before you buy racking. Ask us for the height of the unit you are viewing, then choose shelving that uses it safely, fixed to the wall where the manufacturer says so.
  • Heavy low, light high. Keep heavy stock and machines at or below waist height and light, bulky items on the top shelves.
  • Lockable castors. Benches and rails on lockable wheels let one room switch between making, photographing and packing.
  • One-way flow. Goods in at the door, work in the middle, finished orders back by the door. The 150, 180 and 300 sq ft units have roller shutters; the 160 sq ft unit has a security door.
  • Sockets first. Put machines near sockets and avoid chaining extension leads.

Why our units suit textiles, leather & sewing

  • Fabric, thread and leather are low-risk materials, and sewing, cutting and stitching involve no melting or heating of combustibles.
  • A long cutting table fits comfortably in every unit size, and you can receive clients at the unit for fittings and collections.
  • The 300 sq ft unit gives you a workshop downstairs and an upstairs room with a window for fittings or admin.
  • Lighting, standard single-phase electricity and Wi-Fi are already connected in every unit (there is no three-phase power), with 24-hour access, free CCTV app access covering the entrance and the area outside the units, and shared facilities.

Not allowed in our units

Nothing that melts or heats combustible materials such as waxes, plastics, resins or oils, no flammable liquids or gases in bulk, and no car storage. Low-risk materials such as wood, paper, card, fabric and dry goods are fine. Every business use needs our approval first.

Start-up checklist

  1. Decide which unit suits your stock, machines and how you will load and unload.
  2. Message us with what your business does, so we can approve the use before you commit.
  3. Register your business with HMRC as a sole trader or limited company if you have not already.
  4. Speak to an insurance broker about public liability, contents and stock cover, and employers' liability if you have staff.
  5. Ask us whether business rates apply, and check Small Business Rate Relief on GOV.UK.
  6. Plan your layout: shelving up the walls, benches near sockets, and a clear route to the door.
  7. Arrange one month's rent, the deposit, photo ID and a Direct Debit mandate for move-in day.

Questions

Can I run industrial sewing machines in a unit?

Most industrial sewing machines run on standard single-phase electricity, which is connected in every unit. There is no three-phase power, so check your machines run from a normal plug socket.

Can customers come to the unit for fittings?

Yes. Tenants can receive clients at the unit, and there are shared toilets and a shared tea and coffee room on site.

Is a 150 sq ft unit big enough for a sewing business?

For one or two people, usually yes: room for machines, a cutting table and rails. For curtains or bulky work, look at the 180 sq ft · roller shutter · £78 a week or 300 sq ft · two storeys with upstairs office · £130 a week.

What is included in the rent?

Lighting, electricity and Wi-Fi are already connected in every unit. Electricity usage is charged separately at the supplier rate, with no markup. Tenants get 24-hour access, free CCTV app access covering the entrance and the area outside the units, and shared facilities: a tea and coffee room, toilets and water.

Can I use the address on my Google Business Profile?

Yes. Tenants can use The Old Rock Factory, Keswick Road, Blackpool FY1 5PB as their business address on Google, so customers can find you without you putting your home address online.

Is there three-phase power?

No. Every unit has standard single-phase electricity, the same as a normal plug socket. Choose machines and tools that run on single-phase power.

What can't I do in a unit?

We do not allow anything that melts or heats combustible materials such as waxes, plastics, resins or oils, flammable liquids or gases in bulk, or car storage. Low-risk materials such as wood, paper, card, fabric and dry goods are fine. Every business use needs our approval.
